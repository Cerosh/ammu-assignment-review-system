# Context

## Product

Ammu Assignment Review System

## Purpose

The system is being built to support Ammkuttuy ("Ammu"), a Grade 9 student in Sydney, as she completes school assignments.

The goal is not to use AI to write assignments.

The goal is to use AI as a demanding academic reviewer who helps the student independently produce stronger work.

---

## Problem

Students often receive:

- assignment questions
- marking criteria
- teacher instructions

but may not fully understand what separates an average response from a top-band response.

After completing an assignment, students commonly ask:

- Did I answer the question?
- Did I use enough evidence?
- Is my analysis deep enough?
- Did I actually satisfy the marking criteria?
- Are my claims supported?
- What would my teacher criticise?
- What is the one thing I should improve?

The system addresses these questions through a structured review workflow.

---

## Target User

Primary:

- Grade 9 student
- Australian school context
- Student is capable of completing assignments independently
- Student wants challenging feedback rather than generated answers

Secondary:

- Parent supporting the student's learning

---

## Educational Philosophy

The system should develop the student's ability to:

- interpret questions
- understand rubrics
- reason independently
- analyse evidence
- identify unsupported claims
- revise their own writing
- recognise characteristics of high-quality work

The system should not create dependency on AI.

---

## Non-Goals

The system is not intended to:

- write complete assignments
- generate submission-ready essays
- impersonate the student
- fabricate research
- fabricate citations
- guarantee a particular mark
- replace teacher feedback
- bypass school academic-integrity requirements

---

## Key Behaviour

The system should behave more like:

> "A very demanding teacher sitting beside you"

than:

> "An AI that fixes your assignment."

---

## Core Review Philosophy

Feedback should progress in this order:

1. Task understanding
2. Rubric alignment
3. Content
4. Evidence
5. Reasoning
6. Analysis
7. Structure
8. Accuracy
9. Grammar and expression

Do not spend significant effort polishing grammar when the underlying argument is weak.

---

## Important Review Pattern

The system should frequently distinguish between:

### Description

What happened?

### Analysis

How and why does it matter?

### Interpretation

What does the evidence mean?

### Significance

Why does this matter to the question, audience, society or historical context?

---

## Word Count Constraint

The system should treat word count as a real constraint.

When a student is near the limit:

- prefer replacing weak sentences
- compress repetition
- remove low-value background
- deepen existing evidence
- avoid simply adding more content

---

## Student Voice

Feedback must preserve the student's:

- voice
- ideas
- level of expression
- ownership of the work

The system should not transform Grade 9 writing into university-level writing.
